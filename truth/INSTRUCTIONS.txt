WHY THIS MATTERS

Every accuracy number the project can honestly quote comes from these nine
files. Right now the README says "83.7% mean confidence", which is the
recogniser's opinion of its own work, not a measurement of correctness. It
should not be quoted as accuracy and will be removed once these exist.

Once they exist we get: a real character error rate, separately for prose,
tables and schematics; and an answer to whether deskew, denoise and 300 dpi
actually earn their cost, which is currently argued rather than known.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ONE RULE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Type what is PRINTED on the page. Not what it should say.

If the manual has a typo, keep the typo. If it says "recieve", write
"recieve". You are recording what is on the paper, and the engine is being
marked against the paper.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS MEASURED, AND WHAT IS IGNORED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do NOT spend time on these. They are stripped out before scoring:

  - CAPITALISATION.  Everything is lower-cased before comparison. "WARNING"
    and "warning" score identically. Type whatever is quickest.
  - LINE BREAKS, indentation, blank lines, column alignment. All whitespace
    collapses to single spaces. Do not try to reproduce the layout.

DO get these right. They are compared exactly:

  - PUNCTUATION.  A comma, full stop, colon or hyphen counts.
  - DIGITS AND LETTERS that look alike. 1N21 vs 1N2l vs 1N2I are three
    different answers, and telling them apart is the whole point.
  - SYMBOLS.  Type the real character where you can:
        ohms        -> Omega sign
        micro       -> mu sign
        plus/minus  -> the +/- sign, not "+"
        degrees     -> the degree sign
    In Notepad these paste fine. If a symbol is genuinely unavailable to you,
    see question 3 below and just note it - do not silently substitute, and
    do NOT spell it out as a word.
  - WORD ORDER.  This is scored two ways: once counting order, once ignoring
    it. Getting the order right is worth doing, but if you are unsure which
    of two columns comes first, do not agonise - the second number covers it.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULES FOR THE AWKWARD CASES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. HEADERS AND FOOTERS
   Include them. Running heads ("Model 331A/332A  Section VI") and page
   numbers are printed on the page, so they belong in the text.

2. WORDS SPLIT ACROSS A LINE BREAK
   Join them into one word and drop the hyphen. "replace-" / "able" becomes
   "replaceable". The indexer does this too, so matching it is correct.
   A real hyphen in a compound word ("hand-drawn") stays.

3. SOMETHING YOU CANNOT READ
   Do not guess, and do not leave it out silently. Write exactly:
       [illegible]
   That one token, in square brackets. It will be counted and reported
   separately, and it tells us the ceiling - if a human cannot read 5% of a
   page, no engine can be marked down for missing it. Guessing instead
   poisons the measurement in a way we cannot detect later.

4. TABLES
   Read along the row, left to right, then move to the next row. That is the
   order a person reads a parts list. The seeded text has some rows scrambled
   into column order - fixing that IS the job on these four pages, and it is
   the single most valuable thing in this whole exercise, because reading a
   close-set table along its rows is the specific thing the engine is worst
   at and we have never measured it.

5. SCHEMATICS
   Transcribe every piece of text you can see: component designators (R13,
   C4, A2), values, labels, notes, title blocks. Ignore pure graphics - lines,
   symbols, arrows. If a label is handwritten, transcribe it and mention it in
   your notes.

6. STRUCK-THROUGH OR MARKED-UP TEXT
   The HP-419A page is a marked-up schematic. Transcribe the printed text.
   If something is crossed out by hand, transcribe it anyway and note it.

7. TEXT THAT IS ROTATED OR SIDEWAYS
   Include it, in whatever position you would naturally read it.

8. IF A WHOLE PAGE TURNS OUT TO BE A BAD CHOICE
   Say so in your notes and skip it. I picked these by scoring text density
   and reading the first few lines, which is not the same as having looked at
   them properly. A wasted page is my mistake, not yours.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTIONS I NEED ANSWERED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Please answer these in truth\NOTES.txt - it already has the template, just
fill in the blanks. They are not busywork; each one changes a decision.

PER PAGE (one line each, 10 seconds):

  Q1. MINUTES  - roughly how long did that page take?
      Why: the estimate of 15 minutes a page is a guess, and it decides
      whether growing this set to 30 pages is reasonable or absurd.

  Q2. ILLEGIBLE - how many [illegible] markers did you write?
      Why: this is the ceiling on achievable accuracy for that page. Without
      it a 12% error rate looks like a bad engine when it may be a bad scan.

  Q3. SYMBOLS  - any symbol you could not type? Which one?
      Why: if you substituted or skipped one, the score is wrong in a way I
      would never spot. I would rather fix the file than have a clean-looking
      number.

OVERALL (a few sentences each, at the end):

  Q4. WAS THE SEED WORTH HAVING, OR WOULD A BLANK FILE HAVE BEEN FASTER?
      Answer separately for prose and for tables if they differ.
      Why: I re-seeded these from the reading-order text instead of the
      documented method, on the argument that correcting beats retyping. That
      was my judgement and it is untested. If retyping from blank would have
      been faster for tables, the guide should say so and I should change the
      seeder.

  Q5. DID YOU SEE THE SAME MISTAKE OVER AND OVER?
      Anything like "it always reads 0 as O", "it drops the last letter of
      every line", "it loses everything in bold".
      Why: a systematic error is fixable in code; scattered noise is not.
      This is the single most useful thing you can tell me, and you will see
      it in a way no metric will.

  Q6. ARE THE TWO SCHEMATIC PAGES WORTH KEEPING IN THE SET?
      Honest answer wanted, including "no".
      Why: they hold a few hundred characters of mostly hand-lettered text.
      A character error rate over that little may be too noisy to mean
      anything, in which case the schematic number should be dropped rather
      than quoted. You will know after doing one.

  Q7. ANYTHING THAT MADE YOU THINK THE TASK WAS SET UP WRONG?
      Wrong pages, wrong instructions, something I have not thought of.
